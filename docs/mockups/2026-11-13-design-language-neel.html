Mechanics

  • Sentence case everywhere, titles included. Product and store names as their owners write them.
  • Numbers as digits (3 sizes). ₹ with Indian grouping (₹2,30,000); lakh and crore in words only in sentences (₹2.3 lakh).
  • Dates: Today, Yesterday, then 12 Nov; the year only when not this year. Times: 10:06 pm.
  • Phone: +91 98450 12345. PIN code: 302001. GSTIN in mono.
  • No full stop on a label, button, title or one-line toast; a full stop on sentences.
  • Indian English spelling: colour, cancelled, catalogue.
  • "You" for the brand; "we" for Frrata; "your shopper", never "your customer".

Easy for everyone, on every screen

Accessibility rules

  • Text 4.5:1 or more; large type, lines and field edges 3:1. Text 3 (grey 500) only for big numbers and off states.
  • Focus: a 2 px neel ring on everything you can press, never hidden.
  • Targets 24 px at least; 44 px on a phone.
  • Every field has a visible label; its mistake is tied to it (aria-describedby) and marks it (aria-invalid).
  • Status words, never colour alone. Changes are spoken (toast, "2 selected", moves).
  • Everything by keyboard: Esc closes, Enter confirms the focused action, Ctrl/⌘ S saves, arrows move chips.
  • Reduced motion honoured everywhere. Zoom to 200% without sideways scrolling.
  • Hindi and Tamil names fit: no fixed-width text, Noto fallbacks.

Words

A store owner reads the console on a phone, between orders. Every line should be clear in one read.

The rulebook

  1. One idea, one sentence. Up to 12 words. Two ideas are two sentences, or one is cut.
  2. No joining marks. No colon, semicolon or dash to glue two thoughts together in a sentence.
  3. Everyday words. The words a store owner says out loud. Never ours: tenant, sync, server, token, webhook, variant, readiness.
  4. Say what to do, or what happened. Start with it. "Add a price for Ivory." "Saved."
  5. Buttons are a verb and a thing. "Confirm 2 orders", "Save price". Never "OK", "Submit" or "Proceed".
  6. Errors: what's wrong, then the fix. Next to the field. No "invalid", no "error occurred", no blame.
  7. Numbers, not adjectives. ₹2,490, 8 days, 3 sizes. Dates as "Today, 10:06 pm" or "12 Nov".
  8. Confirm with the consequence only. Title asks with the thing ("Delete 2 drafts?"). Buttons name both outcomes ("Keep them", "Delete 2 drafts").
  9. Law and tax words only where the law needs them. Then an (i) says it plainly.
  10. Calm and direct. "You" for the merchant, "we" for Frrata. No please, kindly, sorry, oops, emoji or exclamation marks.
  11. Easy to translate. No idioms or wordplay. Short sentences carry into Hindi.
