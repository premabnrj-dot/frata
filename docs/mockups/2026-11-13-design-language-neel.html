Words

A shop owner reads the console on a phone, between orders. Every line should be clear in one read.

The rulebook

  1. One idea, one sentence. Up to 12 words. Two ideas are two sentences, or one is cut.
  2. No joining marks. No colon, semicolon or dash to glue two thoughts together in a sentence.
  3. Everyday words. The words a shop owner says out loud. Never ours: tenant, sync, server, token, webhook, variant, readiness.
  4. Say what to do, or what happened. Start with it. "Add a price for Ivory." "Saved."
  5. Buttons are a verb and a thing. "Confirm 2 orders", "Save price". Never "OK", "Submit" or "Proceed".
  6. Errors: what's wrong, then the fix. Next to the field. No "invalid", no "error occurred", no blame.
  7. Numbers, not adjectives. ₹2,490, 8 days, 3 sizes. Dates as "Today, 10:06 pm" or "12 Nov".
  8. Confirm with the consequence only. Title asks with the thing ("Delete 2 drafts?"). Buttons name both outcomes ("Keep them", "Delete 2 drafts").
  9. Law and tax words only where the law needs them. Then an (i) says it plainly.
  10. Calm and direct. "You" for the merchant, "we" for Frrata. No please, kindly, sorry, oops, emoji or exclamation marks.
  11. Easy to translate. No idioms or wordplay. Short sentences carry into Hindi.

Swap these

in order to → to
prior to → before
utilise → use
via → through, by
e.g. → like
commence → start
issue (a bill) → send
respective → (cut)
variant → size or colour
SKU limit exceeded → up to 100
invalid → say what's wrong
successfully → (cut)
is not able to → can't
please note → (cut)
at this time → now
in case of → if
  • Merchants' own trade words stay: COD, RTO, SKU, AWB, GST, HSN, M.R.P.
