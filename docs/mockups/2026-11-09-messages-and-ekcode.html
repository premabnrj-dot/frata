The answer

Keep Messages beside EkCode, not inside it, and build one messaging engine under both.
  • stays the shopper's identity brand and the zero-setup sender: sign-in codes, and order updates for shops that haven't connected a number.
  • Messages is the merchant's own number, in the shop's own name: order and COD messages, the team inbox, cart recovery, broadcasts. Like Interakt, but already wired to the store.
  • Underneath, one system: one contact and consent list per shop, one rule for who sends, one template engine, one send queue, one webhook, one Admin view.

Why not one brand: Meta allows one business per WhatsApp account and forbids speaking for another business, so a shop's marketing can't come from EkCode's number. Every shop's broadcasts would also share one quality rating. No product in the market does it: GoKwik keeps KwikPass and KwikEngage as two apps that share data.

Who sends: one rule, never the merchant's job

One pure function (src/domains/messaging/route.ts). The order page shows which sender was used. A shop that connects its number moves every order update to its own name, with nothing to switch.

One engine under both

One contact list per shop

EkCode's per-shop contact shop_customers is the shop's contact book. Consents per shop (order updates, WhatsApp marketing, email marketing) with notice version and time. Stops per sender. EkCode's network data never used for marketing.

One checkout tick

Worded by sender: "Order updates from Kesar Studio on WhatsApp" when the shop has a number; "…from EkCode on WhatsApp" when not. Never two ticks.

One template engine

Order templates generated from the order's state, checked before sending to Meta (no price, offer or link that makes Meta call it marketing at 7.5× the price), created in the shop's account on connect.

One send queue

One outbox kind whatsapp.send for both numbers; same retries, same 24-hour back-off when Meta holds marketing (131049), same delivered counts.

One webhook

Meta's events routed by number to the shop or to EkCode; the 200-row bug that can starve health events is fixed first.

One Admin view

Admin › EkCode (approved) gains Messages health: shops' numbers, quality, templates paused, Meta spend per shop.

The four options

A · Everything under EkCode No

Breaks Meta's one-business rule for shop marketing; one bad shop could pause broadcasts for all; consent is per shop; shoppers can't tell who writes.

B · Two separate products Works, costly

Two contact lists, two consent models, two template sets, two webhooks, two checkout ticks. The duplication the codebase already shows signs of.

C · One engine, two senders Recommended

EkCode for identity and zero-setup updates; Messages for the shop's own number; everything below shared. Matches how GoKwik and Razorpay split it, without their seams.

D · Resell messages via a Meta Solution Partner Later

Their credit line pays Meta, so the merchant adds no card. But they are the billed party; Frrata still can't add message fees to its invoice. Worth it only if the card step stops merchants.

What merchants complain about, in order: numbers restricted or quality dropping; templates rejected or turned into marketing; wallets, credits and markups; losing the app after moving to the API; slow onboarding; support passed around. Your calls

  1. Option C: Messages beside EkCode, one engine underneath.
  2. EkCode's number in its own Meta business portfolio (same company, verified once more), so a restriction on the merchant side can't stop sign-in, and the daily limits aren't shared. Changes setup step B2.
  3. Billing: Meta bills merchants for their own number now (no markup, no wallet); a Solution Partner only later, if the card step stops merchants.
  4. Plans: inbox and order messages in every plan; cart recovery, broadcasts and ads from Growth.
  5. Order of work: EkCode P1 → M·1 order messages (EkCode's updates ride the same pipeline) → M·2 inbox → M·3 grow → M·4 ads and pay-in-chat. Each phase gets its own mockup.
